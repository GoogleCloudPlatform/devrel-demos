## Brand guidelines

House style for campaign key visuals. Every rule here beats a preference in the
brief. If the brief asks for something forbidden below, follow the brief for the
subject and the guidelines for the treatment.

## Palette

Deep indigo and slate as the ground. One warm accent — warm amber or terracotta —
carrying the eye to the subject. Nothing neon, nothing fluorescent.

At most three colors with any weight. A fourth reads as clutter.

## Light

One dominant source: warm raking golden-hour light. Long shadows are wanted. Golden hour or a
single hard studio key, never flat overhead light and never a ring light.

Rim light is allowed on the subject only, and only to separate it from the
ground.

## Composition

Subject off-center hero, roughly a third into the frame. Shallow depth of field,
with generous negative space on the opposite side — copy lands there later, so leave it genuinely empty.

Eye level or slightly below. No top-down flat lays. No fisheye, no Dutch tilt.

## Subject

One subject: authentic, handcrafted, and human-centered. If the idea implies a crowd, pick the one that carries it.

Photographic realism with a shallow depth of field. Not illustration, not 3D
render, not clay, not pixel art.

## Never

- No neon or fluorescent colors (cool-toned neon cyan or magenta)
- No watermarks, logos, text overlays, or signage anywhere in the frame
- No 3D renders, pixel art, or non-photographic styles
- No collage or split-screen layouts
- No chrome, lens flare, or bokeh sparkle

## Rendering the prompt

When you turn art direction into an image prompt, state the palette, the light,
and the composition explicitly. An image model will not infer them from the
subject alone. Put the "Never" items in as plain description of what the frame
does contain, not as negatives — image models handle a positive description far
better than a list of prohibitions.
